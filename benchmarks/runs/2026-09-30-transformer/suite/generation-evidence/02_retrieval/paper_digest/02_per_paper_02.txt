## Condition monitoring, diagnostics, and degradation indicators
- Dissolved gas analysis (DGA) of transformer oil is the main condition-monitoring technique. It detects incipient faults from gases produced when the insulating oil and paper break down.
- The paper applies several interpretation methods:
  - **Total dissolved combustible gas (TDCG):** four condition levels based on individual gas and total combustible gas concentrations.
  - **Key gas method:** percentage of H2, CH4, C2H2, C2H4, C2H6 and CO; the dominant gas indicates the fault type.
  - **Rogers ratio:** R1 = CH4/H2, R2 = C2H2/C2H4, R5 = C2H4/C2H6.
  - **IEC basic gas ratio:** the same three ratios with different boundaries.
  - **Duval triangle:** relative percentages of CH4, C2H4 and C2H2.
  - **Duval pentagon:** relative percentages of H2, CH4, C2H6, C2H4 and C2H2, located by centroid calculation.
- Combustible gas formation rate: R = (ST − S0) × V × 10⁻⁶ / T, in litres per day. A rate above 2.8 L (0.1 ft³) per day may indicate an internal active fault.
- Case study: a 48 MVA, 33/11 kV Toshiba ONAF transformer with 9,000 L of Idemitsu G oil, sampled on Jan 23, 2020 and Aug 14, 2020.
  - **TDCG:** 967 and 1,081 ppm, which is Condition 2 on both dates.
  - **Ethane (C2H6):** 756 and 791 ppm, which is Condition 4 (above 150 ppm) and indicates deterioration and high damage risk.
  - **Methane (CH4):** rose from 99 to 195 ppm. The paper says CH4 exceeded Condition 1. Note that 99 ppm is below the 120 ppm limit, so only the August value actually exceeds it.
- Results by method:
  - Duval triangle: T1 on both dates (CH4 86–93%, C2H4 7–14%, C2H2 0%).
  - Basic gas ratio: T1, a thermal fault below 300 °C.
  - Rogers ratio: could not identify a fault ("not detected"; R1 of 2.91 and 39 with R5 below 1 match no case).
- Different interpretation methods give different fault indications because they use different gases and different limit values.
- The paper attributes T1 indications to discharge from air trapped in the insulation and to overheating of the conductor-wire insulation.

## Maintenance practices, scheduling, and work-order–relevant context
- The TDCG level and generation rate (ppm/day) set the sampling interval and required action:
  - **Condition 1 (≤720 ppm):** sample yearly if under 10 ppm/day, every 4 months at 10–30, monthly above 30.
  - **Condition 2 (721–1920 ppm):** sample every 4 months if under 10 ppm/day, monthly otherwise. Requires attention, individual gas analysis, and assessment of how loading affects gas formation.
  - **Condition 3 (1921–4630 ppm):** sample monthly if under 10 ppm/day, weekly otherwise. Plan outages and inform the manufacturer.
  - **Condition 4 (>4630 ppm):** sample weekly if under 10 ppm/day, daily otherwise. Above 30 ppm/day, consider replacement and inform the manufacturer.
- The transformer was overhauled and its oil filtered (purified) the day before the Jan 23, 2020 sample. This produced low combustible-gas values.
- Oil filtration reduces combustible gases, but the effect is only temporary. Afterwards the transformer parts, especially the insulation, should be re-checked according to the indicated fault type.
- Maintenance of the Kelman Transport X instrument:
  - Clean the Teflon-coated magnetic stirrers after each test.
  - Replace the in-line Teflon filters every 20 samples.
  - Both filters must always be fitted. If oil reaches the photo-acoustic module, the spectrometer must be replaced.

## Sensor modalities, measurements, and signal interpretation
- The analyser is a portable GE Kelman Transport X. It uses infrared photo-acoustic spectroscopy, with a semiconductor sensor for hydrogen, and also measures moisture.
- Measurement ranges:
  - H2: 5–5,000 ppm
  - CO2, CO, CH4, C2H6 and C2H4: 1–50,000 ppm (CO2 from 2 ppm)
  - C2H2: 0.5–50,000 ppm
  - Water: 0–100%
- Accuracy is ±5% or ±2 ppm for gases, whichever is greater, and ±3 ppm for water.
- Operating conditions are 5–40 °C, up to 2,000 m altitude, and 760–1040 mbar; the rating is IP20.
- The sample bottle lid has an airtight compression fitting and a temperature probe with a humidity sensor, and a magnetic stirrer rotates during analysis.
- TDCG excludes CO2, O2 and N2 because they are not combustible.
- Duval triangle zones:
  - PD: partial discharge
  - D1 and D2: low- and high-energy discharge
  - DT: a mix of thermal fault and discharge
  - T1: below 300 °C
  - T2: 300–700 °C
  - T3: above 700 °C
- Rogers ratio cases range from normal and low- or high-energy arcing to thermal faults below and above 700 °C.
- Duval triangle results were plotted in MATLAB.

## Failure modes, faults, and operational risks
- **Thermal faults:** low-temperature T1 (below 300 °C), T2, T3, thermal-oil and thermal-cellulose faults.
- **Electrical faults:** partial discharge, low- and high-energy arcing or discharge, and corona.
- These faults degrade the oil and insulating paper. The resulting hydrocarbon gases can lead to fires, and continued operation at Condition 4 can damage the transformer.
- In the literature cases reviewed:
  - Dominant H2 indicated partial discharge.
  - C2H2 dominance was reported as a thermal-oil fault, and ratio methods indicated a thermal fault above 700 °C. The paper's attribution of C2H2 to a thermal-oil fault is unusual.
- In the 48 MVA case study, a thermal fault below 300 °C was indicated, attributed to trapped air and overheated conductor insulation.

## Applicable standards, norms, or industry conventions (when mentioned)
- **IEEE Std C57.104:** TDCG, key gas and Rogers ratio. The paper cites both the 2008 and 2019 editions for these; the 2019 edition is also cited for the Duval pentagon.
- **IEC 60599 (2015):** Duval triangle and basic gas ratio.
- The Doernenburg ratio and CO2/CO ratio are mentioned from earlier studies.

## Operator- and manager-style tasks phrased in natural language
- "TDCG went from 967 to 1,081 ppm over about 204 days. What condition is the transformer in, and how often should we resample?" By the paper's tables this is Condition 2 at under 10 ppm/day, so every 4 months. This answer is derived from the tables, not stated in the paper.
- "Calculate the combustible gas formation rate in litres per day for the 9,000 L tank and check it against the 2.8 L/day active-fault threshold."
- "Ethane is at 791 ppm. Which condition level is that, and what actions are required?"
- "Run the Duval triangle and IEC gas ratio methods on the latest DGA sample and report the fault zone."
- "Why did the Rogers ratio method return no diagnosis while the Duval triangle shows T1?"
- "After oil filtration, plan a follow-up inspection of the winding insulation and schedule re-sampling."
- "The Transport X has run 20 samples. Create a work order to replace the in-line Teflon filters."
- "Condition 3 has been reached. Plan an outage and notify the manufacturer."

## Gaps / not supported by this paper
- The Duval pentagon results and the final conclusions are not in the source because the text was truncated.
- No online (continuous) DGA monitoring is covered, and there is no trend analysis beyond two samples.
- Other diagnostics are not covered: furan analysis, moisture results, dielectric tests, and tap-changer or bushing condition.
- The paper gives no confirmed root cause or inspection findings, no maintenance cost or downtime data, and no actual work-order records.
- There are internal inconsistencies:
  - The IEEE C57.104 edition cited varies between 2008 and 2019.
  - The paper states CH4 exceeded Condition 1 on both dates, but the Jan value of 99 ppm is below the 120 ppm limit.
  - Several table titles are mislabelled.
