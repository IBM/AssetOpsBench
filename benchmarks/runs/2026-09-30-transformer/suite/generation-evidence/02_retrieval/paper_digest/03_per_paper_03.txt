## Condition monitoring, diagnostics, and degradation indicators
- Frequency response analysis (FRA) is used to assess the mechanical integrity of the power transformer's active parts, especially the windings.
- FRA is comparative: a reference FRA signature is compared with the present signature, and the deviations between them indicate the transformer's condition.
- Numerical indices quantify the differences between a pair of FRA traces. The paper uses five indices as features for a feedforward backpropagation ANN classifier.
- The ANN classifies six conditions:
  - healthy windings
  - healthy windings with saturated core
  - mechanical deformations
  - shorted turn faults
  - open circuit faults
  - reproducibility issues from different test conditions
- Known limitations of numerical indices:
  - thresholds are not established
  - fixed frequency sub-bands are inappropriate, because sub-band ranges depend on transformer rating and design
- An adaptive frequency division splits the spectrum into four sub-bands, each linked to physical parts:
  - LFB1 and LFB2: core (magnetizing inductance, network capacitance)
  - MFB: inter-winding mutual inductance and capacitance
  - HFB: winding structure (series and ground capacitances)
- The database holds 139 FRA measurements from 80 transformers. Types include GSU, transmission, distribution, shunt reactor, GIS connector and dry-type units.

## Maintenance practices, scheduling, and work-order–relevant context
- Interpreting FRA results currently requires skilled personnel. Automated classification is meant to support transformer asset management decisions.
- Growing electricity demand pushes transformers to higher loading, which increases mechanical, thermal and electrical stress. This motivates winding integrity checks.

## Sensor modalities, measurements, and signal interpretation
- The FRA measurement is a transfer function with magnitude in dB and phase, taken across a frequency sweep. The end-to-end open circuit FRA signature is referenced.
- Two types of noise affect substation FRA measurements:
  - Narrowband noise from the power frequency and its harmonics.
  - Broadband noise from the instrument's noise bed. The noise bed is defined by the instrument's dynamic range.
- Noise appears at low frequency, before the first resonance, as small spurious peaks and valleys.
- Denoising method: a Gaussian moving-average filter with a variable window. It is applied until the number of low-frequency valleys equals the expected number of anti-resonances, which depends on the transformer type and configuration.
- Sub-band boundaries come from resonances, anti-resonances and phase zero-crossings (inductive↔capacitive transitions):
  - Default LFB2 ends are 3, 8 or 15 kHz when no suitable peak is found.
  - Search limits are 10 kHz and 100 kHz for the low-frequency bands, and 30–400 kHz for the MFB.
- FRA traces show 12 features, grouped into three pattern classes by design:
  - Class 1: star-connected windings with accessible neutral, autotransformers, and high-series-capacitance HV disk windings.
  - Class 2: star-connected windings without accessible neutral, MV/LV windings, and generator transformer secondaries.
  - Class 3: delta windings, ordinary-disk or layer windings, and low-impedance windings.
- Helical or ordinary-disk windings have low series capacitance. This produces multiple high-frequency resonances, so their winding region is hard to identify.
- Vector groups such as Dd0 and Dyn0, and autotransformers, cause earlier frequency-slicing methods to fail.

## Failure modes, faults, and operational risks
- A reliability survey of 964 major failures found the winding to be the dominant failure location (38%).
- Fault and condition types covered:
  - mechanical winding deformation
  - shorted turns
  - open circuit faults (electrical faults)
  - core saturation (residual magnetism effect on healthy windings)
- Risk of misdiagnosis:
  - Differences in test conditions can produce FRA deviations that are reproducibility issues, not real faults.
  - Substation noise can distort the low-frequency response.

## Applicable standards, norms, or industry conventions (when mentioned)
- FRA measurement procedures are standardized in the IEC and IEEE standards. Neither provides a reliable interpretation code, and both state that sub-band ranges depend on transformer ratings.
- IEC 60076-18 sets the minimum dynamic range for an FRA instrument at −90 dB to +10 dB.
