## Condition monitoring, diagnostics, and degradation indicators

**Complementary diagnostic pillars**

- **Mechanical condition** is assessed with sweep frequency response analysis (SFRA/FRA). It covers windings, core, clamping structure, leads to bushings, and the tap changer.
- **Electrical and thermal condition** of the insulating oil and paper is assessed with dissolved gas analysis (DGA). Oil and cellulose decompose under partial discharge, arcing, or overheating and release characteristic gases.
- Combining the two gives a fuller health picture of oil-immersed power transformers than either method alone.

**FRA/SFRA fingerprinting**

- FRA is comparative. The present trace (magnitude in dB, phase in degrees) is compared against one of:
  - a baseline fingerprint taken at commissioning or after repair,
  - a sister unit, or
  - other phases of the same unit.
- Deviations indicate winding or magnetic-circuit abnormalities.
- **Correlation-factor indicator (Rxy)** is computed in three fixed bands:

  | Band | Frequency range |
  |---|---|
  | RLF | 1–100 kHz |
  | RMF | 100–600 kHz |
  | RHF | 600 kHz–1 MHz |

- **Severity grading:**
  - **Normal:** RLF ≥ 2.0, RMF ≥ 1.0 and RHF ≥ 0.6.
  - **Slight deformation:** 1.0 ≤ RLF < 2.0, or 0.6 ≤ RMF < 1.0.
  - **Obvious deformation:** 0.6 ≤ RLF < 1.0, or RMF < 0.6.
  - **Severe deformation:** RLF < 0.6.
- **Example grading cases:**
  - A 63 MVA, 110/22 kV YNyn0d11 unit had phase-pair RLF 1.02–1.38, RMF 1.55–1.92 and RHF 1.08–1.36. It was graded as slight deformation. The source is inconsistent about its voltage rating, also listing it as 115/23/11 kV.
  - A 40 MVA, 110/22 kV unit had RLF 2.01–2.3, RMF 2.25–2.6 and RHF 1.85–2.52. It was graded as normal, in agreement with the utility testing centre.
- **Adaptive sub-band interpretation.** Fixed bands can be inappropriate because the physically meaningful ranges depend on rating, winding type and vector group. An alternative splits the spectrum into bands tied to physical parts, with boundaries set from resonances, anti-resonances and phase zero-crossings:
  - **LFB1/LFB2:** core (magnetizing inductance and network capacitance).
  - **MFB:** interaction between windings (inter-winding mutual inductance and capacitance).
  - **HFB:** winding structure (series and ground capacitances).
- **Automated classification** (for example, numerical indices feeding a neural-network classifier) distinguishes six conditions:
  - healthy windings,
  - healthy windings with a saturated core (residual magnetism),
  - mechanical deformation,
  - shorted turns,
  - open circuits,
  - reproducibility issues caused by differing test conditions.
- **Other FRA indicators:**
  - A peak-frequency shift implying a capacitance change can indicate insulation deterioration or geometry change.
  - Tap-changer contact coking appears as a low-frequency magnitude drop (about 20 Hz–2 kHz).
  - Contact pitting appears as a shift toward higher frequencies.

**DGA indicators**

- **Individual gas concentrations** are compared with limits. Typical Condition 1 / single-gas limits cited, in ppm:

  | Gas | Limit (ppm) |
  |---|---|
  | H₂ | 100 |
  | CH₄ | 120 |
  | C₂H₆ | 65 |
  | C₂H₄ | 50 |
  | C₂H₂ | 1 |
  | CO | 350 |

  One source attributes these values to IEC 60599. They also correspond to the IEEE C57.104 Condition 1 table.
- **Total dissolved combustible gas (TDCG/TCG)** gives four condition levels. It excludes CO₂, O₂ and N₂.
- **Key-gas method.** The dominant gas suggests the fault type:
  - H₂: partial discharge.
  - C₂H₂: arcing.
  - C₂H₄: hot oil.
  - CO: overheated cellulose.
- **Ratio methods:**
  - Rogers ratios (CH₄/H₂, C₂H₂/C₂H₄, C₂H₄/C₂H₆).
  - IEC basic gas ratios (the same three ratios with different boundaries).
  - Doernenburg ratios, mentioned from earlier studies.
  - CO₂/CO ratio, used for paper involvement.
- **Graphical methods:**
  - The Duval triangle uses the relative percentages of CH₄, C₂H₄ and C₂H₂.
  - The Duval pentagon uses H₂, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄ and C₂H₂ and locates the fault by centroid.
- **Gas formation rate** is calculated as R = (S_T − S_0) × V × 10⁻⁶ / T, in litres per day. A rate above about 2.8 L/day (0.1 ft³/day) may indicate an active internal fault. Trend and rate usually matter more than a single snapshot.

**Example DGA cases**

- **20 MVA, 110/22 kV unit:**
  - Gases (ppm): H₂ 0, CH₄ 40.43, C₂H₆ 18.24, C₂H₄ 20.92, C₂H₂ 0, CO about 2131, CO₂ about 8933, TCG 2118. The source also gives CO as 2031.18 in one table.
  - CO is far above 350 ppm, which points to cellulose involvement.
  - Diagnosis: thermal fault below 300 °C (T1).
- **48 MVA, 33/11 kV ONAF unit with 9,000 L of oil:**
  - TDCG rose from 967 to 1,081 ppm over about 204 days, which is Condition 2.
  - Ethane was 756–791 ppm, which is Condition 4 (above 150 ppm) and indicates deterioration and high damage risk.
  - Methane rose from 99 to 195 ppm. The source says CH₄ exceeded Condition 1 on both dates, but only the later value exceeds 120 ppm.
  - The Duval triangle and IEC ratios gave T1. The Rogers ratio returned no match.

**Method disagreement**

- Interpretation methods use different gases and limits, so they can disagree. Rogers ratios often fall into "no matching case" zones.
- Good practice is to cross-check several methods and weigh trends.

**Other common indicators (general domain context, not covered by the digests)**

- Oil quality: moisture in oil, dielectric breakdown voltage, interfacial tension, acidity.
- Furan compounds (2-FAL) as a marker of paper ageing and estimated degree of polymerization.
- Power factor / tan δ and capacitance of windings and bushings.
- Winding resistance, turns ratio (TTR), and excitation current.
- Top-oil and hot-spot temperatures, and online DGA trends.

## Maintenance practices, scheduling, and work-order–relevant context

**When to perform SFRA/FRA**

- On every new transformer, to capture the baseline fingerprint.
- After transportation or relocation, and after reinstallation.
- As part of routine electrical testing.
- After through-faults or prolonged downstream short circuits.
- After tap-changer repair.
- After vacuum treatment, oil filtration or oil regeneration.
- After any internal fault or protection trip.
- It is commonly applied to transformers above 6 kV.

**Interpreting FRA results**

- Interpretation currently requires skilled personnel. Automated classifiers are meant to support asset-management decisions.
- Test conditions must be recorded so that later results are comparable:
  - tap position,
  - lead configuration,
  - residual magnetism (for example, after DC winding-resistance tests),
  - grounding.
- Otherwise, reproducibility differences can be mistaken for faults.

**DGA sampling intervals and actions by TDCG condition and rate (IEEE C57.104 practice)**

| Condition (TDCG) | Rate < 10 ppm/day | Rate 10–30 ppm/day | Rate > 30 ppm/day | Required actions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** (≤ 720 ppm) | Yearly | Every 4 months | Monthly | Routine monitoring |
| **2** (721–1,920 ppm) | Every 4 months | Monthly | Monthly | Exercise caution; analyse individual gases; assess load dependence |
| **3** (1,921–4,630 ppm) | Monthly | Weekly | Weekly | Plan an outage; notify the manufacturer |
| **4** (> 4,630 ppm) | Weekly | Daily | Daily | Consider removal or replacement (especially above 30 ppm/day); notify the manufacturer |

**Oil treatment and sampling logistics**

- Oil filtration, purification, degassing or regeneration temporarily lowers dissolved gas levels.
  - A post-treatment sample will look deceptively clean.
  - Record the treatment in the maintenance history.
  - Re-baseline the gas levels.
  - Resample after the gases have re-equilibrated, commonly within weeks to a few months (general practice).
- After oil treatment, the insulation and active parts should still be checked according to the previously indicated fault type, because treatment does not remove the source.
- Oil samples may be analysed by a utility or third-party testing laboratory (gas chromatograph per ASTM D3612) or on site with a portable analyser.

**Portable DGA analyser upkeep (example: photo-acoustic unit)**

- Clean the magnetic stirrers after each test.
- Replace the in-line PTFE filters every 20 samples.
- Always keep both filters fitted. Oil ingress into the photo-acoustic module requires replacing the spectrometer.

**Consequences and typical practice (partly general domain context)**

- Undetected winding deformation or core displacement can lead to costly repair, rewinding or full replacement, plus supply interruption. This justifies early and periodic diagnosis.
- Rising load pushes transformers harder and increases mechanical, thermal and electrical stress. This raises the value of integrity checks.
- Typical practice beyond the digests:
  - Annual offline DGA for large units; many critical units have online DGA monitors.
  - Periodic electrical testing every 3–6 years, or after events.
  - Scheduled OLTC inspection by operation count or time.
  - Bushing power-factor testing.
  - Cooling system maintenance (fans, pumps, radiators).
  - Work orders are raised in a CMMS and prioritized by criticality, health index and test findings.

## Sensor modalities, measurements, and signal interpretation

**SFRA/FRA test setup**

- Injects a low-voltage sinusoid of about 1–10 Vrms.
- Sweeps typically 20 Hz–2 MHz.
- Amplitude = 20·log₁₀(|V_meas|/|V_ref|); phase = ∠V_meas − ∠V_ref.
- **Measurement schemes:**
  - End-to-end open-circuit (EEOC), the most commonly referenced.
  - End-to-end short-circuit (EESC).
  - Capacitive inter-winding (CAP).
  - Inductive inter-winding (IND).
- Comparisons are typically made phase to phase (for example, A-N vs B-N with the other windings open) or against the unit's own baseline.
- IEC 60076-18 requires an instrument dynamic range of at least −90 dB to +10 dB.

**Rxy computation**

1. Compute the variances Dx and Dy and the covariance Cxy of the two traces.
2. LRxy = Cxy / √(Dx·Dy).
3. Rxy = 10 if 1 − LRxy < 10⁻¹⁰; otherwise Rxy = −log₁₀(1 − LRxy).

**Reading FRA traces**

- **Low frequencies** are dominated by the core and magnetizing inductance.
  - They are sensitive to residual magnetism and core saturation.
  - A shifted low-frequency response on otherwise healthy windings often reflects residual magnetism, not damage.
- **Mid frequencies** reflect interaction between windings.
- **High frequencies** reflect winding geometry (series and ground capacitance) and the leads.
- **Trace shape depends on design**, falling into three pattern classes:
  - **Class 1:** star-connected windings with accessible neutral, autotransformers, and HV disk windings with high series capacitance.
  - **Class 2:** star-connected windings without accessible neutral, MV/LV windings, and generator transformer secondaries.
  - **Class 3:** delta windings, ordinary-disk or layer windings, and low-impedance windings.
- Helical or ordinary-disk windings have low series capacitance. They produce multiple high-frequency resonances that are hard to interpret.
- Dd0 and Dyn0 vector groups and autotransformers can defeat simple frequency-slicing rules.

**Qualitative fault signatures in FRA**

- LV winding buckling.
- Twisting or loosened clamping.
- Axial deformation.
- Inter-turn short circuit, which typically changes the low-frequency response noticeably.
- Open circuit.

**Substation noise in FRA**

- **Sources:**
  - Narrowband noise from 50/60 Hz and its harmonics.
  - Broadband noise from the instrument noise floor.
- **Symptoms:** spurious small peaks and valleys before the first resonance.
- **Mitigation:** a Gaussian moving-average filter with a variable window, applied until the number of low-frequency valleys matches the expected number of anti-resonances for the design.
- **Sub-band boundaries** come from resonances, anti-resonances and phase zero-crossings:
  - The default LFB2 end is 3, 8 or 15 kHz when no suitable peak is found.
  - Search limits are about 10 and 100 kHz for the low-frequency bands and 30–400 kHz for the mid band.

**DGA laboratory measurement**

- Gas chromatography (for example, per ASTM D3612 Method C) with about 1 ppm detection.
- Gases measured: O₂, N₂, H₂, CO, CO₂, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄, C₂H₂, C₃H₆ and C₄H₈.
- Units: µL/L (ppm).

**DGA field measurement (portable photo-acoustic analyser)**

- Uses infrared photo-acoustic spectroscopy, with a semiconductor sensor for H₂. It also measures moisture.
- Typical ranges:

  | Measurement | Range |
  |---|---|
  | H₂ | 5–5,000 ppm |
  | CO, CO₂, CH₄, C₂H₆, C₂H₄ | 1–50,000 ppm |
  | C₂H₂ | 0.5–50,000 ppm |
  | Water | 0–100% relative saturation |

- Accuracy is ±5% or ±2 ppm, whichever is greater.
- Operating limits are 5–40 °C and up to 2,000 m altitude.

**Gas-generation patterns**

- **150–500 °C overheating:** mainly H₂ and CH₄, with less C₂H₄ and C₂H₆. Ethane and methane dominance is typical of low-temperature (T1) oil overheating.
- **Rising C₂H₄:** indicates higher-temperature thermal faults (T2/T3).
- **Low-energy discharge:** H₂ and CH₄ with trace C₂H₂.
- **High-energy arcing (about 700–1,800 °C):** a large increase in C₂H₂. Any sustained C₂H₂ rise is a serious flag.
- **High CO and CO₂ (and the CO₂/CO ratio):** indicate cellulose (paper) involvement.
- **Diagnostic zones:**
  - Duval triangle: PD, D1, D2, DT (mixed thermal and discharge), T1 (< 300 °C), T2 (300–700 °C) and T3 (> 700 °C).
  - Rogers cases: normal, low- or high-energy arcing, and thermal faults below or above 700 °C.

**Other common sensors (general domain context)**

- Online multi-gas or hydrogen DGA monitors.
- Top-oil and winding hot-spot temperature, including fibre-optic probes.
- Load current.
- Moisture-in-oil sensors.
- Bushing monitors (capacitance / tan δ).
- UHF or acoustic partial-discharge sensors.
- OLTC motor torque and operation counters.
- Cooling fan and pump status.
- Buchholz relay and pressure-relief alarms.

## Failure modes, faults, and operational risks

**Mechanical faults (mainly detected by FRA/SFRA)**

- Winding deformation: radial buckling (hoop), axial deformation or telescoping, twisting, displacement, and full or localized collapse.
- Loosened clamping pressure.
- Shorted turns (inter-turn short circuits).
- Open circuits.
- Core movement, loose core components, core-joint faults, and core grounding damage.
- Faults in the lead and winding-to-bushing connections.
- On-load tap-changer contact coking and pitting.

**Typical causes of mechanical faults**

- Through-fault short-circuit forces.
- Transport shocks.
- Seismic events.
- Ageing that reduces clamping.

**Electrical and thermal faults (mainly detected by DGA, IEC 60599 classes)**

- **PD:** partial discharge (corona).
- **D1:** low-energy discharge.
- **D2:** high-energy discharge (arcing).
- **DT:** mixed thermal and discharge.
- **T1:** thermal fault below 300 °C.
- **T2:** thermal fault 300–700 °C.
- **T3:** thermal fault above 700 °C.
- Faults may involve oil only or cellulose (paper) as well.

**Example attributions from the case studies**

- T1 faults were attributed to overheated conductor insulation and discharges from air trapped in the insulation.
- High CO accompanying a T1 fault indicates paper overheating.
- One source links C₂H₂ dominance to a "thermal-oil" fault. Conventional practice associates acetylene mainly with arcing or very high-temperature faults, so treat that attribution cautiously.

**Statistics**

- A reliability survey of 964 major failures found the winding to be the dominant failure location (about 38%).

**Operational risks**

- **Progression:** undetected mechanical damage can progress to dielectric breakdown or thermal runaway, causing forced outages, supply interruption, and expensive repair or replacement.
- **Fire:** combustible gas build-up and arcing create fire and tank-rupture risk. Continued operation at DGA Condition 4 risks severe damage.
- **Heavier loading:** increased loading raises mechanical, thermal and electrical stress.

**Misdiagnosis risks**

- FRA deviations caused by residual magnetism or core saturation, test-lead or tap-position differences, or substation noise can be mistaken for faults.
- Conversely, the effect of a real deformation on the trace depends on design and may be subtle.
- DGA methods can disagree or return no diagnosis.
- Recent oil treatment masks gas levels.
- A turns-ratio test can reveal shorted turns but cannot identify discharges or other internal damage.

**Other common failure modes (general domain context)**

- Bushing insulation failure.
- OLTC mechanism and diverter-switch faults.
- Moisture ingress through leaking gaskets or a failed breather.
- Paper ageing (loss of degree of polymerization).
- Cooling system failure leading to overheating.
- Oil leaks and low oil level.

## Applicable standards, norms, or industry conventions (when mentioned)

**Frequency response analysis**

- **IEC 60076-18:** FRA measurement on power transformers. It sets a minimum instrument dynamic range of −90 dB to +10 dB.
- **IEEE FRA guide (IEEE C57.149):** also standardizes FRA measurement practice.
- Neither the IEC nor the IEEE document gives a definitive interpretation code, and both note that sub-band ranges depend on transformer design and rating.
- **DL/T 911-2004 (Chinese power industry standard):** defines the Rxy correlation-factor bands (1–100 kHz, 100–600 kHz, 600 kHz–1 MHz) and the deformation grading. One source describes it as the only standard giving a quantitative comparison of frequency responses.

**Dissolved gas analysis**

- **IEEE C57.104:** DGA interpretation.
  - The 2008 edition covers the TDCG four-condition table, sampling intervals and actions, key gases, Rogers and Doernenburg ratios, and the gas-rate calculation.
  - The 2019 edition adds the Duval pentagon and revised practice.
  - Sources cite both editions, sometimes interchangeably.
- **IEC 60599 (2015):** fault classes PD, D1, D2, T1, T2 and T3; basic gas ratio limits; the Duval triangle; and typical gas concentration values. One source misprints it as "IEC 60559".
- **ASTM D3612 (Method C):** gas-chromatographic determination of dissolved gases in oil.

**Related standards commonly used (general domain context, not cited in the digests)**

- **IEC 60567 / ASTM D923:** oil sampling.
- **IEC 60422:** in-service oil maintenance.
- **IEC 60076-7 / IEEE C57.91:** loading and thermal ageing.
- **IEC 61198 / ASTM D5837:** furan analysis.
- **IEEE C57.152:** field diagnostic testing.
- **CIGRE brochures:** FRA and DGA interpretation guidance.

## Operator- and manager-style tasks phrased in natural language

**FRA / SFRA**

- "Compute RLF, RMF and RHF for the A-N vs B-N SFRA traces of the 63 MVA, 110/22 kV transformer and classify the winding deformation severity."
- "The latest SFRA shows RLF = 1.02 on phase pair C-A. Is that slight or obvious deformation, and should we schedule an internal inspection or a repeat test?"
- "Compare this week's FRA trace for transformer T3 against its commissioning fingerprint and tell me whether the windings look healthy, deformed, shorted or open."
- "The low-frequency band deviates but the mid and high bands overlay. Could this be residual magnetism after the winding-resistance test? Recommend whether to demagnetize and retest."
- "Flag FRA results in the fleet where deviations are likely test-condition reproducibility issues, such as a different tap position or leads, rather than real faults."
- "Denoise this substation FRA measurement and confirm the number of low-frequency anti-resonances expected for a Dyn11 unit."
- "Determine the LFB1, LFB2, MFB and HFB boundaries for this autotransformer's FRA trace."
- "Check whether our FRA instrument meets the IEC 60076-18 dynamic range requirement before the outage campaign."
- "Which transformers need a new SFRA fingerprint after this month's tap-changer repairs, relocations and oil regeneration work?"

**DGA**

- "Given H₂ 0, CH₄ 40, C₂H₆ 18, C₂H₄ 21, C₂H₂ 0, CO 2131 and CO₂ 8933 ppm, which IEC 60599 fault class applies, and is the paper involved?"
- "List the gases in transformer T1's latest oil sample that exceed the single-gas limits."
- "TDCG went from 967 to 1,081 ppm over about 204 days. What IEEE condition is this unit in, and what resampling interval applies?" By the table, this is Condition 2 at under 10 ppm/day, so resample every 4 months.
- "Calculate the combustible gas formation rate in litres per day for the 9,000 L tank and compare it with the 2.8 L/day active-fault threshold."
- "Ethane is at 791 ppm while TDCG is only Condition 2. How should we rate the risk, and which actions are required?"
- "Run the Duval triangle, IEC ratio and Rogers ratio methods on the last two samples and explain why Rogers returns no diagnosis while Duval shows T1."
- "The oil was filtered the day before the last sample. Mark that result as post-treatment, re-baseline, and schedule a follow-up sample and an insulation check."

**Combined assessment, work orders and fleet management**

- "After the through-fault on the 110 kV feeder last night, create work orders for SFRA, DGA, winding resistance and turns-ratio tests on the affected transformer before re-energization."
- "Compare our automated SFRA and DGA diagnoses with the testing-centre reports and list any disagreements for engineering review."
- "Summarize the combined mechanical (SFRA) and thermal/electrical (DGA) health of the 40 MVA unit for the asset manager, including recommended next actions."
- "The 48 MVA ONAF unit shows a T1 thermal fault trend. Check whether the gas increase correlates with load and top-oil temperature, and whether the cooling fans are operating correctly."
- "One unit has reached Condition 3. Plan an outage window, notify the manufacturer, and increase DGA sampling to monthly."
- "Rank the fleet by combined FRA severity grade and DGA condition, and propose which transformers to prioritize for internal inspection in next year's budget."
- "The portable DGA analyser has run 20 samples since the last service. Raise a work order to replace the in-line filters and log the stirrer cleaning."
